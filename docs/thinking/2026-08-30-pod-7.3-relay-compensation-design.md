**Certification (do not re-derive the mechanism):**

**Ratified basis:** `docs/decisions.md` D-POD-KNOBS knob 2 (lines 869-907, AMENDED 2026-08-27).
**Spec home:** `docs/design/pod.md` §7.3 (new, this branch) + §7 build-order item 3.

This note records the PACE deliberation for how relay/gateway bandwidth
compensation is wired. The mechanism shape is already certified: sender-funded
incremental micropayment, PayWord/Orchid form, bilateral, self-enforcing, no
committed state, no TTP, reusing the blind-token machinery. What this note
decides is the *wiring* — chain construction, the increment/redeem flow, where
it reuses the shipped machinery, and where it plugs into the serve/redeem path.
It also scopes the one owed quantitative gate (the increment size) as a
measurement, not a guess.

---

## 1. The problem, stated concretely

The neutral lane (§3) prices a *completed* delivery: a fetcher retrieves an
object, content-verifies it, and hands the server a signed receipt that redeems
into a conserved balance credit. That works when the payer knows delivery
succeeded before paying — the fetcher verifies bytes against the content address
(tenet B3), then signs.

A relay/gateway is content-blind. It forwards bytes it cannot verify toward a
fetcher. There is no completed-delivery receipt to sign, because the relay never
holds a verifiable object; it holds a stream of forwarded bytes. Two facts from
the certification bound the design:

- **No transit proof exists.** For the relay to be *owed* payment for forwarding
  increment N it would have to prove it forwarded N — unprovable by any mechanism
  (cert §2c; Tor's proof-of-bandwidth line failed, endpoint attestation dies
  under endpoint collusion).
- **TTP-free atomic fairness is impossible** (Pagnia–Gärtner). No mechanism, TTP
  or not, makes forward-then-pay atomic.

So the design cannot be "prove forwarding, then settle." It must be "pay as you
go, in increments small enough that the irreducible one-increment stiff is
cheap." That is exactly what a sender-funded micropayment chain is.

## 2. The options (how relay compensation is wired)

### Option A — PayWord hash chain, sender-funded, per-session (CHOSEN)

The fetcher builds a hash chain `x_0 = H(x_1), x_1 = H(x_2), … , x_S = H(x_{S+1})`
by picking a random tip `x_{S+1}` and hashing S+1 times. The **root** `x_0`
(the value reached by hashing the most times) is committed once to the relay,
bound to a blind credit worth `S · increment`. To authorize the k-th increment
the fetcher reveals `x_k`; the relay verifies `H^k(x_k) = x_0` (in practice it
verifies each new preimage one hash from the last one it holds: `H(x_k) =
x_{k-1}`). The relay redeems the highest preimage it holds for `k · increment`
at settlement.

- **Cost:** cheapest possible per-increment verify — one SHA-256. Chain state is
  `S · 32 B` at the fetcher, `1 × 32 B` (the last preimage) at the relay. Reuses
  the shipped blind-credit withdrawal wholesale (`client/privissue.go`,
  `AcquireDemandTokenWithCredit`). Self-authorizing: the relay cannot forge a
  preimage (one-way hash), the fetcher cannot deny a revealed one.
- **Benefit:** exactly the certified shape (cert VERDICT, Q2). Scales best as
  increments shrink (verify is O(1) per increment regardless of chain length).
  No new crypto primitive — SHA-256 is already a core dependency.
- **Residual:** the irreducible one-increment stiff in either direction. Bounded
  small by the increment-size measurement (§5). Not a defect — a priced residual
  (cert §4 item 5).

### Option B — per-increment blind token (one token per increment)

Each increment is its own blind-withdrawn demand token; the fetcher hands one
token per increment forwarded.

- **Cost:** a blind RSA withdrawal + verify *per increment*. At ~KB increments
  over a large object this is thousands of RSA operations — orders of magnitude
  more expensive than one SHA-256. Also multiplies issuer round-trips.
- **Benefit:** reuses the demand-token verify path with zero new construction.
- **Rejected:** RSA-per-increment does not scale as increments shrink, which is
  the whole point of bounding the stiff small. PayWord's O(1)-hash verify is why
  the literature (and the cert, Q2) picks it.

### Option C — Orchid-style probabilistic nanopayment (lottery tickets)

Each increment is a lottery ticket with win probability `p` and payout
`increment/p`; the relay banks only winning tickets.

- **Cost:** removes per-increment settlement I/O (only winners settle), at the
  cost of variance. Needs a verifiable-randomness/commit-reveal construction the
  repo does not have.
- **Benefit:** lowest settlement-side bookkeeping for very high increment counts.
- **Rejected for v1:** it buys settlement-I/O savings we do not yet need (relay
  credit settles into the operator balance at epoch net-settlement granularity,
  not per-increment — cert Q5), and it adds a randomness-beacon dependency and
  variance the certified PayWord shape avoids. The cert names Orchid as prior art
  for "variance is the cost," not as the chosen mechanism. Revisit only if
  settlement I/O becomes the measured bottleneck.

### Option D — quorum-TTP adjudicated relay dispute (REJECTED — ratified out)

A committee adjudicates relay disputes.

- **Rejected — and this is not a builder call, it is ratified.** D-POD-KNOBS
  knob 2 (AMENDED 2026-08-27) and the 2026-08-30 cert both establish there is no
  adjudicable relay dispute: the payment self-verifies, forwarding is unprovable,
  so a quorum has nothing to adjudicate. A TTP here adds trust surface and buys
  nothing. Recorded so the spec stops re-raising it.

**Decision: Option A (PayWord, sender-funded, per-session-fresh).** It is the
certified shape, the cheapest verify, reuses the shipped blind-credit machinery
whole, and adds no new primitive or trust surface. The rejected options are
recorded so a future reader sees they were weighed, not missed.

## 3. The increment / redeem flow (Option A, concretely)

The flow reuses the shipped engine; the new elements are the chain construction
and the relay-side highest-preimage tracker.

1. **Fetcher withdraws a blind credit under a FRESH EPHEMERAL identity.** Reuses
   `client.WithdrawDemandTokenPrivately` (`client/privissue.go:48`) — the D3
   slice-1 path already built and verified present. The credit is worth
   `S · increment`; the issuer learns neither the fetcher's durable identity nor
   the chain. This is where invariant (i) is enforced by construction (§4).
2. **Fetcher builds a fresh PayWord chain and commits the root to the relay.**
   `x_0` is bound to the blind credit in a session-open message. One chain per
   session (invariant (ii), §4). The relay stores `(root x_0, credit, k=0)`.
3. **Fetcher pays as bytes forward.** After the relay forwards increment k, the
   fetcher reveals `x_k`. The relay checks `H(x_k) = x_{k-1}` (the preimage it
   currently holds), advances its held preimage to `x_k`, and sets `k`. One
   SHA-256 per increment. If the fetcher stops revealing, the relay stops
   forwarding (self-enforcing; the stiff is bounded to one increment).
4. **Relay settles at epoch net-settlement.** The relay redeems its highest held
   preimage `x_k` for `k · increment` into its **operator balance** — the same
   committed-state path delivery credit rides (cert Q5), no new keystone field.
   Conservation holds: the credit is drawn from the fetcher's already-paid blind
   credit, never minted (cert §2a).

### Where it plugs into the current path

- **Withdrawal / privacy:** `client/privissue.go`
  (`WithdrawDemandTokenPrivately`) — reused as-is for the ephemeral blind credit
  that funds the chain root.
- **Settlement into balance:** the relay-credit redeem is the sibling of
  `core/credit/delivery.go` `RedeemDeliveryCredit` — it moves `balance` only,
  drawn from the fetcher's paid-in credit, never touches `Reputation()`
  (`core/credit/credit.go:291-298`). One Invariant-A regime covers delivery +
  relay (cert §2b, Q6). A new `RedeemRelayCredit(relay, fetcher, chainRoot,
  increments)`-shaped entry point settles the highest-preimage value; it composes
  exactly as the delivery redeem (conserved transfer, optional skim).
- **The chain / preimage verify:** a new `core/credit` (or `core/relaypay`)
  primitive holds the PayWord construction (build S+1 hashes) and the relay-side
  verify (`H(x_k) = x_{k-1}`). SHA-256 only; no new dependency. This is the one
  genuinely new code unit; the rest is reuse.
- **The consumer gate:** the relay-accept path mirrors the `--accept-receipts`
  gate at `cmd/silt/daemon.go:810` (`EnableDemandBank`). A relay opts into
  accepting PayWord chains under an analogous flag; off by default.
- **The serve/demand role:** `core/node/demandrole.go` is the model — the relay
  side banks and settles the same way `handleDeliveryReceipt`
  (`demandrole.go:175`) banks and settles a delivery receipt, but keyed on the
  chain root and the highest revealed preimage instead of a completed-delivery
  receipt.

The relay is a distinct role from the delivery server: a delivery server holds
the object and signs nothing; a relay forwards and verifies preimages. But both
settle into the operator balance through the identical conserved-transfer,
firewall-safe path. That is the reuse the certification relies on.

## 4. The two M0 invariants — enforced by construction

These are non-negotiable build constraints from the certification's must-not-trade
residuals (cert §4 conditions 1+2), grounded in M0 access-privacy — an immutable
bright line (`docs/TENETS.md` Don't-#3). They are written into the §7.3 spec as
load-bearing constraints, not notes.

**(i) The PayWord chain root binds to a BLIND credit under a FRESH EPHEMERAL
identity, never a durable one.**

- **Enforced by:** funding the chain exclusively through
  `client.WithdrawDemandTokenPrivately` (`client/privissue.go:48`), which spins up
  a throwaway keypair, withdraws over it, and tears it down. The issuer
  authenticates only an ephemeral key and charges no account it can tie to the
  fetcher. The blind signature hides the serial; the ephemeral identity severs
  the account/identity link. There is no code path that binds a chain root to a
  durable-account credit.
- **Why it may not be traded:** binding a chain to a durable identity turns the
  relay into a party that can tie the fetcher's durable identity to what it
  fetched — an M0 access-privacy violation, not permitted at any performance
  price.

**(ii) A fresh ephemeral identity AND a fresh chain per session.**

- **Enforced by:** one `WithdrawDemandTokenPrivately` call (one ephemeral
  identity) and one freshly-generated PayWord tip per session-open. No chain or
  ephemeral identity is cached or reused across sessions. The relay stores only
  `(root, credit, highest-preimage)` for the live session and discards it at
  settlement.
- **Why it may not be traded:** reusing a chain or ephemeral identity across
  sessions lets the relay link a fetcher's sessions to each other — it upgrades
  the relay from a per-session observer (which sees only the IP it already routes)
  to a **longitudinal** observer. That is a real Don't-#3 regression.

**How the spec shows enforcement:** §7.3 states both invariants as MUST
constraints, names the exact function that satisfies (i), and requires a
failing-first guard for each — a session-open that reuses an ephemeral identity
or a chain root must be rejected, and a chain funded by a durable-account credit
must be rejected. (Tests are the builder's next step, not this one.)

## 5. The owed measurement — the increment size (build-immutable #8)

**The increment size is a MEASUREMENT in BYTES, not a round figure** (cert §4
item 3, the #299/#555 produce-cost scar). This note does NOT pick a value. It
defines precisely what must be measured and the method a Tester runs.

### What to measure

The increment size `B` (bytes of payload per PayWord increment) is the value that
satisfies two floor-box constraints simultaneously:

- **(a) Verify overhead ≤ ~1% of forwarding time.** The per-increment cost is one
  SHA-256 over 32 bytes (the preimage check `H(x_k) = x_{k-1}`) plus the
  bookkeeping to advance the held preimage. `B` must be large enough that this
  per-increment cost is ≤ ~1% of the time to forward `B` payload bytes on the
  floor box's network path. Too-small `B` makes verify dominate; too-large `B`
  widens the one-increment stiff.
- **(b) Chain state stays MB-scale even for large objects.** The fetcher holds
  `S · 32 B` where `S = objectSize / B`. For the largest object class the network
  serves, `S · 32 B` must stay MB-scale. This is a lower bound on `B`. The relay
  holds only `1 × 32 B`, so (b) is a fetcher-side memory constraint.

The measured `B` is the smallest value satisfying both (a) and (b), then aligned
to a sub-chunk boundary (the cert's ~1–64 KiB range is the expected envelope, not
the answer). Smaller `B` is better for the stiff bound, so the binding constraint
is (a) — the point where verify overhead would exceed ~1%.

### The two numbers the measurement produces

1. **Per-increment overhead**, in nanoseconds and in bytes:
   - *time:* one SHA-256(32 B) + preimage-advance bookkeeping, benchmarked on the
     1 vCPU / 2 GB floor box.
   - *bytes:* the wire cost of one increment authorization = one revealed preimage
     (32 B) + framing. This is the per-increment protocol overhead that competes
     with `B` payload bytes.
2. **Forwarding time per `B` payload bytes** on the floor box's representative
   path, so ratio (a) can be evaluated.

### Method (what a Tester would run)

1. **Micro-benchmark the verify** (`go test -bench`, no `-race` for timing — the
   memory-profile-under-race caveat from session-6): time `H(x_k) = x_{k-1}` plus
   the preimage-advance on the floor-box hardware profile (1 vCPU / 2 GB, the same
   box class the store measurement used). Produces the per-increment verify
   nanoseconds.
2. **Measure forwarding throughput** on the floor box's representative network
   path (the relay's forward leg) to get time-per-`B`-bytes across candidate `B`
   in the 1–64 KiB range.
3. **Solve for `B`:** the smallest `B` where verify ns ≤ 1% of forward-time(`B`)
   AND `objectSize_max / B · 32 B` ≤ the MB-scale fetcher-memory budget. Align to
   a sub-chunk boundary.
4. **Report the two numbers + the chosen `B`** with the measurement artifact
   (bench output + the box profile), the same evidence discipline the store
   measurement followed. No round figure ships without this artifact.

This is the one quantitative gate the builder owes before committing relay-payment
code. It does not reopen the design (cert §4 item 3).

## 6. The held-in-tension residual (disclosed, not solved)

The irreducible one-increment stiff (cert §4 item 5): the fetcher can stop paying
after the relay forwards one unpaid increment, or the relay can stop forwarding
after being paid for one. Not eliminable by any mechanism (Pagnia–Gärtner +
forwarding-unprovability). The remedy is to bound the increment small (§5), NOT to
adjudicate. The spec says this plainly.

**Skim on relay payment — an engineering knob, deferred.** Whether to apply a
skim on relay settlement (deterrent vs simplicity) is conservation-safe either
way; it composes exactly as the delivery skim (`delivery.go:120-124`). v1 ships
without a relay skim for simplicity — the conservation bound already makes wash a
strict non-gain, and adding a skim is a one-line follow-on if a deterrent floor is
later wanted. Recorded so it is a known knob, not a silent omission.

## 7. Summary of the decision

- **Mechanism:** Option A — sender-funded PayWord hash chain, per-session-fresh,
  root bound to a blind ephemeral credit, redeemed into the operator balance at
  epoch net-settlement. The certified shape; cheapest verify; reuses shipped
  blind-credit machinery whole; no new primitive, no TTP.
- **Two M0 invariants enforced by construction:** (i) chain root funded only via
  `WithdrawDemandTokenPrivately` (ephemeral blind credit, never durable); (ii) one
  ephemeral identity + one chain per session, never cached or reused. Each gets a
  failing-first guard.
- **Owed measurement:** the increment size `B` in bytes, solved from
  (a) verify ≤ ~1% of forward time and (b) chain state MB-scale, measured on the
  1 vCPU / 2 GB floor box. Method above. No round figure ships.
- **Doc true-ups landing with §7.3:** §5 Q3 stale "dispute-only quorum-TTP …
  owner's call held" → "NO TTP, self-enforcing"; §2 line 49 "inert" → shipped
  consumer at `cmd/silt/daemon.go:810`.
