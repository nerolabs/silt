The machinery is banked and tested in isolation: the PayWord chain math, the
conserved `RedeemRelayCredit` settlement with its per-call budget cap, the two
M0 session-open guards, the CLI gate. What is missing is the wire: the fetcher
never sends a session-open, no preimage crosses the network, the forwarding loop
never gates on a reveal, and the relay never settles a live session. This note
decides that wiring. It does NOT reopen the certified mechanism.

The PE ruling names three tracked IOUs the transport increment must discharge,
and the coupling that ties two of them together:

> The conservation bound and the DoS bound are the SAME missing object: the
> committed chain budget S. Carry S into the session and enforce `count <= S`,
> and both the DoS clamp and the conservation cap fall out of it.

This note treats that coupling as the spine of the build order (§7).

## 1. The on-wire session protocol

### The decision

Add three message kinds that mirror the delivery-receipt lane exactly, and
register them in the same `switch msg.Kind` dispatch in `core/node/node.go` where
`MsgDeliveryReceipt` routes to `handleDeliveryReceipt`
(`node.go:1577`). The relay side is a sibling of `demandrole.go`
`handleDeliveryReceipt` — verify, advance/bank, ack.

| New kind | Direction | Payload | Mirrors |
|---|---|---|---|
| `MsgRelayOpen` | fetcher → relay | CBOR `RelayOpen{Root, S, Funding, EphID-proof}` | (no delivery analogue — this is the fund/commit step) |
| `MsgRelayOpenAck` | relay → fetcher | `OK` + session handle | `MsgDeliveryReceiptAck` |
| `MsgRelayPay` | fetcher → relay | CBOR `RelayPay{Session, Preimage, Count}` | `MsgDeliveryReceipt` (the spend) |
| `MsgRelayPayAck` | relay → fetcher | `OK` (+ current authorized count) | `MsgDeliveryReceiptAck` |

Settlement needs NO wire message. Per the certification (Q5) and the machinery
note §3, the relay settles into its own operator balance at epoch net-settlement
by calling `RedeemRelayCredit` locally against the highest preimage it holds. The
fetcher already paid at blind withdrawal; there is no on-wire "settle" handshake
to run, and adding one would be a linkage surface for nothing (see §6). So the
protocol is open + pay + local-settle — three kinds, not four.

### Why mirror the delivery lane rather than invent a stream protocol

- **The dispatch already exists.** `handleDeliveryReceipt` is the exact shape:
  a request kind, a verify-or-deny, a bank-and-settle, an ack. `handleRelayPay`
  drops into the same `switch` with the same `deny()` / `reply(OK)` structure.
  Registering a new kind is a one-line `case` plus the `String()` and the
  `isReply` set entry in `ports/net.go` (the delivery kinds show every site:
  `net.go:149-150`, `net.go:281`, `net.go:296`).
- **One message struct, flat fields.** silt's wire is one `ports.Message` with a
  `Kind` discriminator and a `Data` CBOR blob (`ports/net.go:114,191`). The three
  new kinds carry their structs in `Data`, exactly as `MsgDeliveryReceipt` carries
  `demand.SubmittedReceipt`. No new framing, no new transport.

### Where MsgRelayOpen differs from a delivery receipt

A delivery receipt is a completed-delivery ack: one message, one bank, done. A
relay session is stateful — the relay holds a live `RelaySession` (the
`relaypay.Verifier`) between `MsgRelayOpen` and the last `MsgRelayPay`. The node
needs a per-session table keyed by a session handle the `MsgRelayOpenAck`
returns. This is the one genuinely-new state the transport adds; §4 bounds it.

### Options weighed

- **Option A (CHOSEN): three request/reply kinds on the existing message
  dispatch.** Reuses `n.request`/`n.reply`, the CBOR-in-`Data` convention, and the
  `handleDeliveryReceipt` structure. Cost: one live session table on the relay.
  Benefit: zero new transport, and the reviewer reads it against a known-good
  sibling.
- **Option B: piggyback the preimage on the relay adapter's byte stream**
  (`adapters/relay/server.go` splice), inline with the forwarded bytes. Rejected:
  it entangles the content-blind splice (which forwards opaque bytes between two
  swarm peers and keeps no state, `server.go:48-49`) with payment parsing. The
  relay would have to parse the stream it is deliberately blind to. That breaks the
  splice's "keeps no state beyond live connections" property and puts payment logic
  in the byte pump. The payment channel must be a SEPARATE control exchange
  (`MsgRelayPay`), out-of-band from the spliced bytes — see §2.
- **Option C: a bespoke long-lived payment stream protocol.** Rejected as
  gold-plating: the request/reply dispatch already carries everything, and a
  fresh stream protocol is new surface with no traceable requirement behind it.

## 2. The forwarding loop

### The decision

The relay gates forwarding on authorized increments. The `splice` byte pump
(`server.go:344-357`) currently forwards up to `MaxSessionBytes` unconditionally
via `io.Copy(dst, io.LimitReader(src, MaxSessionBytes))`. The transport replaces
the flat `MaxSessionBytes` limit on the PAID direction with a
payment-gated limit: the relay forwards up to `count * RelayIncrementBytes` bytes,
where `count` is the highest increment the session's verifier has authorized, and
advances that ceiling as `MsgRelayPay` messages arrive.

Concretely: the paid-direction pump reads from a `LimitReader` whose limit is
raised each time `Verifier.Advance` succeeds. "Pay as you go" is: the relay
forwards increment k only after it holds `x_k`. If the fetcher stops revealing,
the pump blocks at the current ceiling and the relay stops forwarding. The
irreducible stiff is bounded to one increment (`RelayIncrementBytes` = 4096 B),
exactly the certified residual (machinery note §6).

### The mechanism, precisely

- The payment channel (`MsgRelayPay`) is OUT-OF-BAND from the spliced bytes. The
  fetcher sends `MsgRelayPay` on its control conn to the relay; the relay advances
  the verifier and raises the pump's byte ceiling. The spliced content bytes flow
  on the separate spliced pipe. This keeps the splice content-blind (§1 Option B).
- The pump gate: instead of one `io.LimitReader(src, MaxSessionBytes)`, the paid
  direction forwards in increments. A per-session `authorizedBytes` counter =
  `verifier.Count() * RelayIncrementBytes`; the pump forwards until it reaches
  `authorizedBytes`, then waits (a condition variable or a buffered channel
  signaled on each `Advance`). `MaxSessionBytes` stays as the hard outer cap: the
  session can never forward more than 1 GiB regardless of payment (it is the same
  ceiling that bounds S — see §3).
- Stop condition: on session close, EOF, or the fetcher going silent past a
  deadline, the pump stops and the relay settles the highest held preimage.

### Where it plugs in

`adapters/relay/server.go`. The relay adapter is a distinct layer from
`core/node`; the payment verifier lives in `core/node` (the `RelaySession` in
`relayrole.go`). So the transport needs a thin seam: the relay adapter's paid pump
consults an authorization source that the node owns. Two options:

- **Option A (CHOSEN): the node drives, the adapter gates.** The node owns the
  `relaypay.Verifier` (it already does — `RelaySession.verifier`, `relayrole.go:79`).
  The relay adapter exposes an `authorizedBytes` hook the node updates on each
  `Advance`. The pump reads the current authorization through that hook. Cost: one
  callback/shared-counter seam between adapter and node. Benefit: verification and
  M0 guards stay in `core/node` (the tested, ruled-on layer); the adapter stays a
  dumb byte pump that only knows "how many bytes am I cleared to forward."
- **Option B: move the verifier into the relay adapter.** Rejected: it duplicates
  the M0 guards and the S-clamp into the adapter layer, off the tested path, and
  splits the security-load-bearing logic across two packages. The PE ablated all
  five load-bearing checks in `core/node`/`core/credit`; keep them there.

### Note on the existing splice's coupling

`splice` closes BOTH conns on the first EOF (`server.go:349-350`), because "swarm
exchanges are short-lived." A paid relay session for a 1 GiB object is NOT
short-lived. The transport increment must NOT reuse the both-close-on-first-EOF
pump for paid sessions unchanged — a paid forward is a long unidirectional stream,
not a frame-and-reply. This is a real behavioral fork the build must handle: paid
sessions get a pump that forwards the paid direction to its authorized ceiling and
does not tear down on the reverse direction's EOF. Flag for the reviewer; the
short-lived assumption is load-bearing in the current splice and does not hold
here.

## 3. Gate #644 — the DoS clamp (the sharpest hazard)

### The decision

Carry the committed chain length S into the relay-side verifier and clamp every
walk to it. This is the PE's H-1, coupled with the conservation cap (§5) because
both are downstream of the one missing object: S.

- `NewVerifier(root)` becomes `NewVerifier(root, S)`; the `Verifier` stores S.
- `AdvanceTo(preimage, claimedCount)` rejects `claimedCount > S` BEFORE the walk.
  The walk is then bounded to at most `S - count` hashes, and S is itself bounded
  by how it is derived relay-side (below).
- `OpenRelaySession(ephID, root, S, funding)` already takes S (`relayrole.go:93`)
  and already rejects `S <= 0` (`relayrole.go:110`). The transport must also
  reject an S the relay is unwilling to serve — an S larger than
  `MaxSessionBytes / RelayIncrementBytes`. That is the ceiling clamp.

### How S is known relay-side (the exact bound)

S is derivable relay-side WITHOUT trusting the fetcher's claimed S, because S is
pinned on both ends by the session byte cap:

```
S_max = MaxSessionBytes / RelayIncrementBytes = (1 GiB) / (4 KiB) = 262,144
```

`MaxSessionBytes` is the relay's own config (`server.go:23,40`, default 1 GiB);
`RelayIncrementBytes` is the protocol constant (`payword.go:40` = 4096). So the
relay computes `S_max` from values it already holds and clamps the accepted S:

- **At `OpenRelaySession`:** reject `S > S_max`. A fetcher cannot open a session
  claiming a chain longer than the relay will ever forward. This caps the stored S
  at 262,144.
- **At every `AdvanceTo`:** reject `claimedCount > S` (the stored, already-clamped
  value). The walk is then at most `S - count <= 262,144` hashes — the worst case
  is ~262K SHA-256 (~23 ms at ~88 ns/op), not the ~5M (~0.28 s) or ~2^31 the PE
  measured on the unbounded path. Bounded, and bounded by a value the relay chose,
  not the attacker.

The clamp value is `S`, and `S` is `min(fetcher-claimed-S, S_max)`. The
attacker-controlled number (`claimedCount` in a bogus `MsgRelayPay`) can no longer
drive work past `S_max` hashes per message. Combined with the per-session
`MaxSessions` cap (`server.go:34`, default 128), total attacker work is bounded.

### Why the clamp is at BOTH open and advance

Clamping only at open is insufficient: `AdvanceTo`'s `claimedCount` is a
per-message attacker int. Clamping only at advance (`claimedCount <= S`) works if
S is stored and trusted-bounded, which is why open must clamp S to `S_max` first.
Both clamps are needed; they are cheap (two integer comparisons).

### The failing-first test that leads this build

`TestAdvanceToClampsToChainLength` (or the transport-level
`TestRelayPayOversizedCountBounded`): construct a verifier with S=8, send an
`AdvanceTo`/`MsgRelayPay` with `claimedCount = 5_000_000` and a bogus preimage,
assert it is REJECTED and that the walk executed at most `S` hashes (instrument a
hash counter, or bound wall-clock well under the 0.28 s the PE measured). RED
before the clamp (the PE's measured ~5M-hash spin), GREEN after. This test leads
the increment (§7 step 1).

## 4. Gate #645 — map eviction

### The decision

Evict `relaySeenEph` / `relaySeenRoot` on an EPOCH boundary, not by raw FIFO. The
eviction trigger is epoch rotation: when the chain's epoch advances, drop the seen
entries older than the current epoch.

### Why epoch-tied, not FIFO

The PE ruling (H-2) and issue #645 both name the trap: a raw FIFO evict of an old
root is a guard-(ii) regression — if an evicted root is re-admitted, a relay could
be made to re-accept a chain it already saw, re-opening the cross-session linkage
the guard forecloses. So eviction cannot be "forget the oldest to make room." It
must be "forget entries that can no longer be replayed against a live session."

An epoch boundary is the natural TTL because the M0 design already ties freshness
to per-session identity. Bound the seen-set to the current epoch window: an
ephemeral identity and a chain root are single-use within an epoch, and the seen
entry only needs to outlive the sessions that could reuse it. Concretely:

- Tag each `relaySeen*` entry with the epoch in which it was admitted.
- On epoch rotation, drop entries tagged with an epoch older than the retention
  window (one full epoch back, so a session opened just before a boundary is still
  protected across the boundary).
- The retained window is bounded: `sessions-per-epoch * 2 epochs` entries, not
  unbounded growth at the session rate.

### How the epoch is known node-side

The node already tracks epochs on-chain: `Chain.Head()` gives the head height
(`chain.go:2443`), `EpochBlocks` is the epoch length (`chain.go:200`), and
`epochStart` is the current epoch's boundary height, exposed on the snapshot
(`chain.go:2161,2169`). The relay-accept path already requires the chain to be
useful (the daemon wires `EnableRelayAccept` alongside chain features). So the
eviction trigger hangs off the same epoch-rotation signal the chain already
computes in `rotateEpoch` (`chain.go:3387`).

Two candidate triggers:

- **Option A (CHOSEN): drive eviction from the chain's epoch on each
  `OpenRelaySession`.** On session open, read the current epoch (from
  `Chain.Head()` height / `EpochBlocks`, or the snapshot's `EpochStart`). If it
  advanced since the last sweep, evict stale-epoch entries then. Cost: an epoch
  read + occasional sweep on the open path. Benefit: no new timer goroutine, no new
  chain callback; eviction is lazy and deterministic (swept when the accept path is
  exercised, which is exactly when the map grows).
- **Option B: a chain epoch-rotation callback (like `OnReorg`,
  `chainrole.go:558`).** Rejected for v1: it adds a new chain→relay hook and a
  goroutine-safety surface for eviction that the lazy open-path sweep achieves
  without a callback. Revisit only if a relay must evict while idle (no opens) for
  a long stretch — an unlikely operational shape.

### The reorg caveat (flag for reviewer)

`epochStart` is reorg-swapped (it is an observable under no committed root — see
the machinery lineage). A reorg that moves the epoch boundary backward could, in
principle, un-advance the eviction epoch. This does NOT create a guard-(ii) hole
in the safe direction: the seen-set is a REJECT filter, so a reorg that makes it
retain MORE (evict less) only over-rejects, never under-rejects. A reorg that
makes it retain LESS is the risk. Mitigation: key eviction off a monotonic
high-water epoch (never decrease the eviction floor on a reorg). Name this in the
spec; the safe rule is "eviction floor only moves forward."

### The failing-first test

`TestRelaySeenMapEvictsOnEpoch`: open sessions across a simulated epoch advance,
assert entries from two-epochs-ago are gone AND that a root from the CURRENT and
PREVIOUS epoch is still rejected on reuse (guard (ii) still holds across the
boundary). RED before eviction (map grows unbounded — assert size stays bounded)
and RED if eviction is too aggressive (a same-epoch reuse slips through). This is
the ablation the PE demands: inject both the unbounded-growth defect and the
too-eager-evict defect and watch each redden.

## 5. Session-level budget accumulation (conservation across the session)

### The decision

Single settlement at session close, against the committed budget. The relay
settles ONCE per session — at close — redeeming the highest preimage it holds for
`count * RelayIncrementBytes'` credit value, capped at the committed budget
`S * increment`, itself capped at the fetcher's paid-in blind credit. This is the
certified shape (machinery note §3 step 4; cert Q5: settlement at epoch
net-settlement granularity, not per-increment).

### Why single-settlement-at-close, not per-call accumulation

The per-call cap in `RedeemRelayCredit` (`relay.go:69`, `chainValue > budget`
rejects) is a backstop against ONE over-redeem. It does not, by itself, stop a
relay from calling `RedeemRelayCredit` multiple times across a session and
summing past the budget — each call individually passes `chainValue <= budget`
while the sum exceeds it. The live session must enforce "redeem the highest
preimage ONCE."

- **Option A (CHOSEN): settle once at close.** The session holds the verifier; at
  close the node calls `RedeemRelayCredit(relay, fetcher, count*incrementCredit,
  budget)` exactly once. The `count` is monotonic (the verifier only advances), so
  the single settlement at close IS the highest authorized value. No accumulation
  bookkeeping — the verifier's monotonic `count` is the accumulator. This is the
  simplest thing that enforces the bound, and it matches the certified
  "highest-preimage-at-settlement" shape.
- **Option B: per-`MsgRelayPay` incremental settlement with a running
  session-total cap.** Rejected: it settles N times per session (I/O per
  increment — the exact cost the certification rejected Orchid/per-increment for,
  cert Q2/Q5), and it needs a session-total accumulator that single-settlement
  gets for free from the verifier's monotonic count. More code, more I/O, same
  bound. Only revisit if a relay must bank partial credit before close for
  crash-safety — a durability concern not in scope for v1.

### The enforcement chain (the PE's IOU #5)

The consumer (the transport's close path) enforces the full cap chain the PE named:

```
chainValue = count * RelayIncrementBytes' credit value
           <= committed budget (S * increment)      [S clamped at open, §3]
           <= fetcher's paid-in blind credit          [the withdrawal amount]
```

- `count <= S` is guaranteed by the §3 clamp.
- `S * increment <= paid-in` is guaranteed at `OpenRelaySession`: the relay must
  verify the committed budget the chain root is bound to matches a blind credit
  worth at least `S * increment`. This is the funding-proof the `MsgRelayOpen`
  carries. A fetcher cannot open a chain of length S bound to a credit worth less
  than `S * increment`.
- `RedeemRelayCredit`'s existing `chainValue > budget` cap (`relay.go:69`) is the
  ledger-side backstop if the consumer miscomputes.

The failing-first test: `TestRelaySessionCannotRedeemPastBudget` — open S=10, pay
to count=10, attempt to settle for count=11's value (or settle twice), assert the
relay never receives more than `S * increment` and never more than the fetcher
funded. RED if the session settles per-call without the single-settle discipline.

## 6. M0 guards on the LIVE path

### The decision

The two M0 invariants are enforced at `OpenRelaySession` (guard (i) durable-funding
reject; guard (ii) fresh-eph + fresh-root reject) and the live forwarding/settlement
path introduces NO new linkage surface. Confirmed, with one residual flagged.

### Confirmed: no new linkage on forward or settle

- **Forwarding (`MsgRelayPay` + the paid pump):** the preimage reveal carries no
  identity — it is a 32-B hash preimage plus a session handle. The session handle
  is per-session (fresh per `MsgRelayOpen`), so it links `MsgRelayPay` messages
  only WITHIN one session, which is exactly the per-session observation the guard
  permits (the relay already routes that session's bytes). It does not link across
  sessions.
- **Settlement (local `RedeemRelayCredit` at close):** settlement moves credit
  from the fetcher's blind-ephemeral balance to the relay's operator balance
  (`relay.go:57-58`). The fetcher side is the ephemeral identity the blind
  withdrawal produced — never the durable one (guard (i)). The relay learns the
  ephemeral payer, which it already routes bytes for. No new correlation.

### The residual: timing / settlement-metadata correlation (FLAG for reviewer)

A relay CAN observe two things a passive network router could not:

1. **Session timing.** When each session opens/closes and how many increments it
   forwarded (the object size class). This is inherent to being the forwarder —
   the same information a Tor relay sees. It is NOT new to this increment; the
   relay sees it the moment it forwards bytes. But the transport makes it
   PRECISE (increment count = object size / 4 KiB). Flag: does per-increment
   granularity leak object size more precisely than the raw byte stream already
   does? Assessment: no — the relay already forwards the bytes and can count them;
   the increment count is derived from the same bytes. No NEW leak, but name it so
   the reviewer confirms the increment granularity does not sharpen a
   size-fingerprint beyond what raw forwarding already exposes.

2. **Settlement correlation across sessions under ONE relay operator.** A relay
   operator settles many sessions into one operator balance. The settlement amounts
   and timing are visible to the operator. Could the operator correlate two
   sessions as "same fetcher" via settlement metadata? Assessment: no, IF guard
   (ii) holds — each session uses a fresh ephemeral identity and fresh chain, so the
   settlement records are unlinkable ephemeral payers. The linkage would require
   breaking the ephemeral-identity freshness, which guard (ii) forecloses at open.
   The residual to flag: confirm the settlement record stored/logged at close does
   NOT carry any durable-identity field or any cross-session-stable value beyond the
   (already-per-session) ephemeral payer. The `logf` at settlement must not log a
   correlatable durable field — audit the log line the way session-6's
   observable-log-contract lesson demands.

Neither residual is a new linkage the guards do not already bound, but both are
named for the blind reviewer to confirm on the live path (the PE reviews the
artifact, not this rationale).

## 7. Build decomposition

The spine is the PE's coupling: S closes both the DoS clamp and the conservation
cap. Land the gate-closers FIRST (they are pure `core/relaypay` + `core/node`
changes, no wire), then the wire, then the pump. Each step failing-first.

### Step 1 — carry S + clamp AdvanceTo (#644) + budget cap (#645-adjacent)

Pure `core/relaypay` / `core/credit` / `core/node`, no transport.

- `NewVerifier(root, S)` stores S; `AdvanceTo` rejects `claimedCount > S`.
- `OpenRelaySession` clamps `S <= S_max = MaxSessionBytes / RelayIncrementBytes`.
- The single-settlement budget-cap chain (§5).
- **Failing-first tests lead:** `TestAdvanceToClampsToChainLength` (the PE's ~5M-hash
  spin → bounded), `TestRelaySessionCannotRedeemPastBudget`.
- This step alone closes #644 and the PE's IOU #5, and is mergeable without any
  wire. It is the sharpest hazard, so it lands first.

### Step 2 — epoch-tied map eviction (#645)

Pure `core/node`, no transport.

- Tag `relaySeen*` entries with their admit-epoch; sweep stale epochs on open,
  monotonic eviction floor.
- **Failing-first test leads:** `TestRelaySeenMapEvictsOnEpoch` (both the
  unbounded-growth and too-eager-evict ablations).
- Closes #645. Mergeable without any wire.

### Step 3 — the wire protocol (session-open + pay messages)

- Add `MsgRelayOpen`/`MsgRelayOpenAck`/`MsgRelayPay`/`MsgRelayPayAck` to
  `ports/net.go` (kind consts, `String()`, `isReply` set).
- `handleRelayOpen` / `handleRelayPay` in `core/node`, mirroring
  `handleDeliveryReceipt`; register in the `node.go` dispatch.
- The fetcher side: `OpenRelaySession`-sender + `SubmitRelayPay`, mirroring
  `SubmitDeliveryReceipt` (`demandrole.go:152`).
- **Failing-first tests:** a round-trip open+pay+settle over the in-memory
  transport, asserting `count` advances and settlement is conserved; a
  guard-(i)/(ii) reject over the wire (durable funding rejected on the wire, root
  reuse rejected on the wire).

### Step 4 — the forwarding loop (the paid pump)

- The relay-adapter seam (§2 Option A): a payment-gated pump that forwards the
  paid direction to `count * RelayIncrementBytes`, raised on each `Advance`, hard-
  capped at `MaxSessionBytes`, and does NOT tear down on the reverse EOF (the
  long-stream fork, §2).
- **Failing-first tests:** the fetcher stops revealing → the relay stops forwarding
  within one increment (the bounded-stiff assertion); a full paid forward of a
  multi-increment object settles for exactly the forwarded byte count's credit.
- This is the last step because it depends on 1–3 and is the most integration-heavy
  (it touches the adapter's live splice).

### Why this order

- **Gate-closers first (1, 2):** they are the PE's must-close-before-transport
  IOUs, they are pure-core with no wire, and they are the cheapest to test
  failing-first. Landing them first means the sharp CPU-DoS and the unbounded map
  are closed before any message can reach them.
- **Wire before pump (3 before 4):** the pump depends on the session table and the
  authorization source the wire establishes. The wire is testable over the
  in-memory transport without the adapter; the pump is the adapter-integration
  step. Splitting them keeps step 4's larger surface reviewable against a
  known-good step 3.
- Each step is independently mergeable and independently failing-first, so a
  reviewer sees one hazard closed per PR, not a monolith.

## 8. Summary of the decision

- **On-wire protocol:** three request/reply message kinds
  (`MsgRelayOpen`/`Pay`/their acks) on the existing `ports.Message` dispatch,
  mirroring the `handleDeliveryReceipt` lane. Settlement is LOCAL at close, no wire
  message. The payment channel is out-of-band from the content splice (the splice
  stays content-blind).
- **#644 clamp:** carry S into `NewVerifier(root, S)`; reject `claimedCount > S`
  at every `AdvanceTo` and clamp accepted `S <= S_max = MaxSessionBytes /
  RelayIncrementBytes = 262,144` at open. S is known relay-side from the relay's
  own config and the protocol constant — never trusted from the fetcher. Worst-case
  walk drops from ~5M hashes to ~262K. A failing-first oversized-count test leads
  the build.
- **#645 eviction trigger:** epoch rotation, swept lazily on `OpenRelaySession`,
  with a monotonic eviction floor (reorg-safe). Not raw FIFO — a re-admitted root
  is a guard-(ii) regression, so eviction is epoch-TTL, not make-room.
- **Session-budget enforcement:** single settlement at close, capped by the
  committed budget `S * increment` and the fetcher's paid-in blind credit; the
  verifier's monotonic `count` is the accumulator, so no per-call summation can
  exceed the budget.
