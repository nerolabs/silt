## The architectural finding that governs Batch 3

The relay `Server` (`adapters/relay`) and the `Node` (`core/node`) are wired
**independently** in the daemon today. Read `cmd/silt/daemon.go`:

- Line 566: `relay.Serve(*relayServe, ident, relay.Config{}, obs)` starts the relay
  Server. It gets `ident`, a `Config`, a logger — and NO reference to the node.
- Line 820: `nd.EnableRelayAccept()` turns on the node's payment lane. The node
  never sees the relay Server.

The relay Server's splice path (`server.go` `connect` → `accept` → `splice`) forwards
raw ciphertext bytes between two swarm peers and "keeps no state beyond live
connections" (`server.go:47-48`). It has zero knowledge of PayWord, sessions, or the
node's `relaySessions` table. The node's `RelaySession` (the `Authorizer`) is created
by `handleRelayOpen` (a wire message on the node's own swarm transport) and lives in
the node's session table keyed by a `handle`.

**So Batch 3's core problem is a rendezvous:** when the relay Server is about to
splice two conns for a PAID session, it must find the node-owned `Authorizer` for
that session and hand it to `SplicePaid` instead of running the free `splice`. The
relay Server and the node are in the same process (same daemon), but there is no
seam between them today. Batch 3 builds that seam.

This finding reframes items 2 and 3 below: the "paid marker" is how the relay Server
learns a connect is paid; the "Authorizer wiring" is the in-process seam by which the
relay Server looks up the node's `RelaySession` for that connect. They are two halves
of one rendezvous.

## 1. Free-vs-paid routing policy (the load-bearing decision)

### The finding: the design does NOT specify a routing policy. It is OPEN.

I read `pod.md` §7.3 in full, the transport design, and the mechanism cert. All three
specify the *mechanism* (sender-funded PayWord, per-session-fresh, conserved
settlement, the M0 guards) and the *economics* (bandwidth is unpriced today, and the
free-rider/recentralization choke is Phase 4's purpose — `pod.md` §7.3 opening, cert
§1). None of them specifies what a relay does when `--accept-relay-payments` is on and
a FREE swarm relay connect arrives. That is a routing/policy question the transport
increment surfaces for the first time, because Batch 3 is the first place free and
paid relay share one listener.

The economics that DO bind the answer (from the cert and spec, not invented here):
- Relay bandwidth is **unpriced** today. A `-relay` node forwards free, capped by
  `MaxSessions`/`PerPeerSessions`/`MaxSessionBytes` (`server.go:20-24`). This is the
  "rate-capped, opt-in" guardrail, explicitly "not a real abuse story (that's flagged
  for launch)" (`server.go:16-19`).
- The purpose of §7.3 is to give a relay operator a **reason to carry bandwidth it is
  not paid for today**, closing the free-rider/recentralization choke (cert §1, the
  "different operators" framing; `pod.md` §7.3 opening).
- Payment is **sender-funded** and **conserved**: a paid session moves credit the
  fetcher already paid at blind withdrawal into the operator balance. It is never a
  mint (cert §2a).

### Options

**Option A — paid REPLACES free.** With `--accept-relay-payments` on, the relay
refuses free swarm relay and serves only paying fetchers.
- Cost: breaks the universal NAT-fallback contract. The relay lane (`adapters/relay`,
  #27) is the universal NAT fallback: two home-NATed peers meet at a reachable third
  node (`wire.go:1-9`). If enabling payments turned off free relay, every NATed peer
  that cannot pay (no blind credit provisioned, older client) loses reachability. That
  regresses M0-adjacent connectivity for a bandwidth-pricing feature — the wrong trade.
- Benefit: simplest operator story ("this relay is paid, period"); strongest incentive
  to fund. But it is a connectivity cliff.

**Option B — paid is a PARALLEL opt-in path; free relay is UNCHANGED.** The fetcher
chooses paid by opening a paid session (the paid marker); everything else stays free
and rate-capped exactly as today. A relay with `--accept-relay-payments` on serves BOTH:
free swarm relay under the existing caps, and paid sessions on top.
- Cost: a relay could still be drained on the free path (unchanged from today — the
  free caps are the same guardrail, and abuse is "flagged for launch" already). Paid
  and free coexist under the same `MaxSessions` fan-out cap.
- Benefit: no connectivity regression — free NAT-fallback keeps working for everyone.
  Payment is purely additive: a fetcher that wants priority/uncapped forwarding opts in
  and funds it; a fetcher that does not gets today's free, capped relay. This matches
  the certified economics exactly: the payment gives the operator a REASON to carry
  MORE bandwidth (the paid sessions) without removing the free floor. Conserved,
  sender-funded, additive.

**Option C — COEXIST with free rate-limited and paid uncapped.** Free relay stays but
under a TIGHTER cap; paid sessions get a higher/uncapped ceiling. The payment buys you
out of the free rate limit.
- Cost: introduces a NEW economic knob (the free-vs-paid cap ratio) that is not
  certified and is a live economic-mechanism parameter — it changes what free relay a
  node offers, which is a durability/reachability lever. That is exactly the kind of
  "a durability knob was twice also a security parameter" trap `build-process.md`
  warns about. It also means turning on payments SILENTLY degrades free relay for
  everyone, a surprising operator side-effect.
- Benefit: sharper incentive to pay (free is worse), still no hard connectivity cliff.

### Recommendation: Option B (parallel opt-in, free unchanged), and FLAG for the human.

Option B is the simplest thing that matches the certified economics and does not
regress connectivity. Payment is additive: the free, rate-capped NAT-fallback floor is
untouched, and a paying fetcher opts into a paid session for forwarding the free caps
would otherwise throttle. It introduces NO new economic knob (unlike C) and NO
connectivity cliff (unlike A). The existing `MaxSessions`/`PerPeerSessions`/
`MaxSessionBytes` caps continue to bound the free path exactly as today; a paid session
is a distinct, node-authorized splice that the operator chose to serve because it is
compensated.

**Why this is flagged for the human (economic-mechanism policy — the silt research
gate).** "What a relay does with free vs paid traffic" is a D-DEMAND-adjacent economic
policy: it decides whether enabling payments changes the free bandwidth a node
contributes to the network. Option B says "no — free stays, paid is additive." That is
a policy call, and the CLAUDE.md research gate routes economic-mechanism decisions to
the Researcher for certification and the human for ratification. The mechanism cert
certified the *payment mechanism*; it did not rule on the *free-vs-paid coexistence
policy* because that surfaces only now, at the shared listener. **I recommend Option B
and route it: Researcher confirms it introduces no conservation/firewall regression and
no new economic knob (I believe it does not — it changes no credit flow, only which
splice path a connect takes), and the human ratifies the policy.** Batch 3 does not
ship the binding until that verdict is filed. This is the one point in Batch 3 that
may deserve ratification; everything else is mechanism-wiring under an already-certified
design.

**Fallback if the human wants a sharper incentive:** Option C is the only other
conservation-safe choice, but it adds an economic knob and must go through the full
research gate as a parameter, not a wiring detail. I do not recommend opening that knob
for v1.

## 2. The "paid" frame marker

### The decision (pending the §1 verdict): a new optional field on the connect frame.

The relay Server learns a connect is paid from a marker on the EXISTING connect
control frame. The relay control frame is `ctrl` (`wire.go:50-60`), a CBOR struct with
`omitempty` fields. Add one optional field:

```go
type ctrl struct {
    Op     string `cbor:"o"`
    Target []byte `cbor:"t,omitempty"`
    Stream uint64 `cbor:"s,omitempty"`
    Err    string `cbor:"e,omitempty"`
    Addr   string `cbor:"a,omitempty"`
    Paid   uint64 `cbor:"p,omitempty"` // NEW: nonzero = a paid session handle
}
```

`Paid` carries the **session handle** the node returned in `MsgRelayOpenAck`
(`relaytransport.go:52`, the ack's `Height`). Zero (the default, omitted on the wire)
means a free swarm relay connect — today's behavior, byte-for-byte. Nonzero means
"this connect is the byte-stream leg of paid session `Paid`; look up its Authorizer."

### Why a handle on the connect frame, not a separate paid-connect op

- **Backward-compatible by construction.** `omitempty` means an old client's connect
  frame (no `Paid` field) decodes to `Paid == 0` = free. No existing free relay path
  changes. The fuzz corpus (`fuzz_test.go`) and the existing relay tests
  (`relay_test.go`) keep passing unchanged because they never set `Paid`.
- **One dispatch, one code path.** `handle` (`server.go:139-173`) already switches on
  `fr.Op`. Rather than a new `"connect-paid"` op (a parallel path to keep in sync), the
  existing `case "connect"` reads `fr.Paid`: zero → the current free `s.connect`;
  nonzero → the paid variant. Same frame, same op, one branch.
- **The handle is the rendezvous key.** The node already minted the handle in
  `handleRelayOpen` and returned it to the fetcher. The fetcher puts it on the connect
  frame. The relay Server uses it to find the node's `RelaySession` (§3). The handle is
  the shared token that ties the node's payment session (established over the swarm
  wire) to the adapter's byte splice (established over the relay control conn). No new
  correlation state — the handle already exists on both ends.

### The ordering the fetcher follows (already built, this just sequences it)

1. Fetcher blind-withdraws a credit under a fresh ephemeral identity
   (`client.WithdrawDemandTokenPrivately`) — already built.
2. Fetcher sends `MsgRelayOpen` over the swarm wire; relay's `handleRelayOpen` runs
   the M0 guards + S-clamp and returns `handle` in `MsgRelayOpenAck.Height` — already
   built (`relaytransport.go:25-53`).
3. Fetcher dials the relay control conn and sends `connect{Target: origin, Paid:
   handle}` — the NEW marker. The relay Server sees `Paid != 0`, looks up the
   Authorizer (§3), and on the eventual splice runs `SplicePaid` not `splice`.
4. Fetcher reveals preimages via `MsgRelayPay` over the swarm wire as bytes forward —
   already built.

Steps 1, 2, 4 are shipped. Step 3's marker is the only new wire surface, and it is
one optional CBOR field.

### The M0 audit on the marker (design §6 discipline)

The `Paid` handle is a per-session, node-local integer with no cross-session-stable
meaning — it is a table index minted per open and discarded at settlement. It carries
no ephemeral or durable identity and no chain root. It does not weaken the M0 residual
audit (the settlement log discipline, `relaytransport.go:108-112`). Flag for the blind
reviewer: confirm the handle is not logged in a way that correlates two sessions (it is
per-session and monotonic, so logging it is equivalent to logging a session sequence
number — not a fetcher-linking field, but name it for the audit).

## 3. Authorizer wiring — the adapter/node seam

### The decision: a narrow lookup callback the node registers on the relay Server.

The relay Server needs to resolve a paid handle to a node-owned `Authorizer` at splice
time, and needs the node to authorize that THIS authenticated fetcher owns that handle.
The adapter must stay a dumb byte pump (transport design §2 Option A: the verifier and
M0 guards stay in `core/node`). So the seam is a single function value the daemon
installs on the Server, pointing into the node:

```go
// in adapters/relay: a resolver the daemon installs so the paid path can find
// the node-owned authorizer for a handle. The adapter calls it; it does not know
// what a RelaySession is.
type PaidResolver func(fetcher ports.NodeID, handle uint64) (Authorizer, bool)

func (s *Server) SetPaidResolver(r PaidResolver) { ... }
```

- The daemon wires it once, after both the relay Server and the node exist
  (`cmd/silt/daemon.go`, near line 566/820): `rs.SetPaidResolver(nd.ResolveRelayAuthorizer)`.
- `Node.ResolveRelayAuthorizer(fetcher, handle) (relay.Authorizer, bool)` looks up
  `n.relaySessions[handle]`, checks `sess.ephID == fetcher` (the SAME ownership check
  `handleRelayPay` already enforces, `relaytransport.go:76`), and returns the session
  (which already implements `relay.Authorizer` — `RelaySession.AuthorizedBytes/Wait/
  Done`, `relayrole.go:203-218`) or `false`.

### The flow at splice time (the rendezvous completes)

In `server.go` `accept` (`server.go:301-330`), when a parked paid connect is matched:

1. `connect` stashed `Paid` (the handle) and the fetcher's authenticated `from` on the
   `pendingSplice`.
2. At `accept`, before splicing, the Server calls `s.paidResolver(fetcherID, handle)`.
   - `false` (no such session, or the fetcher does not own the handle): refuse the
     splice with an `err` frame — a paid connect that does not resolve to a live,
     owned session is rejected, never silently downgraded to free (a free downgrade
     would let a non-paying fetcher get an uncapped forward).
   - `true`: run `s.SplicePaid(target, p.src, conn, auth)` instead of `s.splice(...)`.
3. When `SplicePaid` returns (forward stream complete), the Server tells the node to
   settle: a second callback, or the node settles on the next `MsgRelayPay` ack / an
   idle timer. **Decision: the node settles at close, driven by the pump's return.**
   `SplicePaid` returns the forwarded byte count; the Server calls a
   `PaidSettler(handle, forwarded)` callback → `nd.SettleRelaySession(handle)`. Single
   settlement at close (design §5), now driven by the LIVE pump's completion instead of
   a test.

### Why a resolver callback, not a shared table or moving the verifier

- **The verifier stays in `core/node`.** The adapter never sees the PayWord verifier,
  the budget, or the M0 guards. It gets an `Authorizer` interface (three methods:
  ceiling, wait, done) and a byte count back. This is exactly transport design §2
  Option A ("the node drives, the adapter gates"); the PE ablated all five load-bearing
  checks in `core/node`/`core/credit` and they must stay there.
- **No new shared mutable state.** The resolver is a read of the node's existing
  `relaySessions` table under the node's existing serialization (the table is "touched
  only from the serialized event loop", `node.go:674`). Flag for the reviewer: the
  resolver is called from the relay Server's accept goroutine, NOT the node's event
  loop — so the lookup + the `ephID` check must be safe against concurrent session-table
  mutation. Either post the lookup onto the node's loop (as the wire handlers do) or
  guard the table with the mutex the session already carries. The build must not read
  the map from the accept goroutine without synchronization; this is a real concurrency
  seam the reviewer must confirm.
- **The daemon owns the wiring.** Neither package imports the other for this: the
  adapter defines the `PaidResolver`/`Authorizer` interfaces; the node satisfies them;
  the daemon connects them. Same dependency direction as the existing `onConn`/
  `RelayInbound` seam (`client.go:69`, daemon line 1138).

## 3b. Reaper teardown — closing the `TODO(Batch-3)`

### The decision: `sweepRelaySeen` calls `sess.closeSession()` on every reaped session.

Today `sweepRelaySeen` (`relayrole.go:129-159`) reaps a stale session's TABLE entry but
the comment records the gap (`relayrole.go:150-153`): once the daemon binding wires
`SplicePaid`, a reaped session may have a LIVE pump goroutine blocked in
`paidPump` waiting on `auth.Wait()`. Deleting the table entry alone would leak that
goroutine — it would block forever on a channel no one signals.

The fix is one line at the reap site:

```go
for handle, sess := range n.relaySessions {
    if sess.admitEpoch < newFloor {
        sess.closeSession() // NEW: mark done + wake the pump so it drains and stops
        delete(n.relaySessions, handle)
    }
}
```

`closeSession()` (`relayrole.go:232-237`) sets `done = true` and wakes the pump. The
pump's gate loop (`paid.go:76-80`) checks `auth.Done()` on each wait and returns when
it is set. So a reaped session's pump drains to its current authorized ceiling and
returns; the session settles for what it forwarded (the `PaidSettler` path, §3) and the
goroutine exits. No leak.

### Why this is safe and correct

- **`closeSession` is idempotent** (`wake()` is a non-blocking send on a depth-1 buffered
  channel, `relayrole.go:239-244`). Calling it from the reaper and again from the normal
  close path is harmless.
- **A reaped session settles for the honest count.** The verifier's monotonic count is
  unchanged by the reap; the pump forwarded only up to the authorized ceiling. Settling
  the reaped session (rather than dropping it) pays the relay for the bytes it actually
  forwarded before the reap — conserved and correct. Flag: confirm the reaper's settle
  path and the normal-close settle path cannot BOTH settle the same handle (double
  settle). The `delete` under the node's serialization plus the "settle removes the
  handle" discipline (`SettleRelaySession` deletes, `relaytransport.go:101`) should make
  the second settle a no-op (handle absent), but the reviewer must confirm the reaper and
  the pump-completion settler race safely on one handle.

### The failing-first test

`TestReapedSessionStopsLivePump`: open a paid session, start its pump blocked on an
unmet ceiling, advance the epoch past the retention window, run the sweep, assert the
pump goroutine RETURNS (does not leak) and the session is gone from the table. RED
before the `closeSession()` line (the pump blocks forever — assert with a timeout that
the goroutine exits) and green after. This is the ablation the `TODO` names.

## 4. The e2e proof

### The test: a real node-to-node paid relay session over the network.

`e2e/relay_paid_test.go` (a sibling of the existing relay/delivery e2e in
`e2e/e2e_test.go`), three real daemons over loopback TCP:

- **Origin (B):** a daemon that holds an object, NATed (registers with the relay so it
  is reachable only through it) — mirrors the existing relay e2e setup.
- **Relay (R):** a daemon started with `-relay ADDR --accept-relay-payments`. It offers
  free relay AND accepts paid sessions (Option B).
- **Fetcher (S):** a daemon that blind-withdraws a credit under a fresh ephemeral
  identity, opens a paid session to R (`MsgRelayOpen`), gets a handle, dials R's relay
  control conn with `connect{Target: B, Paid: handle}`, and fetches the object through
  the paid splice, revealing preimages (`MsgRelayPay`) as bytes forward.

### What it asserts

1. **Bytes forward end-to-end.** The fetcher reconstructs the exact object B holds
   (the forward stream is not corrupted by the paid gate) — the `paid_test.go` TCP
   assertion, now over three real daemons and the full connect/accept/splice path.
2. **Pay-as-you-go gates on the LIVE path.** If the fetcher stops revealing preimages,
   the relay stops forwarding within one increment (the bounded stiff) — assert the
   forward stalls at `count × increment` until the next `MsgRelayPay`.
3. **Settle-at-close is conserved.** After the session closes, R's operator balance
   rose by exactly `count × RelayIncrementCredit`, drawn from the fetcher's paid-in
   blind credit — never more than `S × increment`, never more than the fetcher funded
   (the conservation cap, design §5). Assert R's `Reputation()` is UNCHANGED (the
   Invariant-A firewall — relay credit is balance-only, cert §2b).
4. **Free relay still works with payments on (Option B).** A second, non-paying
   fetcher reaches B through R's free relay under the existing caps — proving payments
   are additive, not a replacement (the §1 policy, made observable). This assertion is
   the e2e witness for the routing-policy decision.
5. **The M0 log audit on the live path.** R's settlement log line carries no durable or
   cross-session-stable field (the `waitForInLog` discipline from session-6; the
   `relaytransport.go:108-112` audit, now exercised end-to-end).

### Why e2e, not just integration

The connect/accept/splice rendezvous (§3) only exists across the adapter/node seam in a
real daemon. The pump was proven over raw TCP in `paid_test.go` and the wire over the
in-memory transport in `relaytransport_test.go`, but the RENDEZVOUS — the paid marker
on the control frame resolving to the node's Authorizer at splice time — has no unit
home. It is a daemon-integration behavior, so it needs a daemon-integration test. This
is the test that would have caught "SplicePaid is reachable only from tests."

## 5. Build decomposition + failing-first plan

Four steps, each failing-first, each independently reviewable. The rendezvous (the
adapter/node seam) is the risk, so it lands in the middle with the marker beneath it and
the e2e on top.

### Step 0 (GATE) — file the §1 routing-policy verdict

Before any code: route the free-vs-paid policy (Option B) to the Researcher for a
no-regression certification and the human for ratification. Batch 3's binding does not
ship until the verdict is filed (CLAUDE.md research gate + veto gate on economic
policy). This is a gate, not a code step. Proceed on the filed verdict, not a verbal
summary.

### Step 1 — the reaper teardown (§3b)

Pure `core/node`, no adapter, no wire. Add `sess.closeSession()` at the reap site.
- **Failing-first:** `TestReapedSessionStopsLivePump` (the pump-leak ablation).
- Closes the `TODO(Batch-3)`. Mergeable alone; it hardens the existing session table
  independent of the binding. Land it first because it is the cheapest and it removes a
  latent leak the moment the pump goes live.

### Step 2 — the paid marker on the connect frame (§2)

Pure `adapters/relay`, no node. Add the `Paid` field to `ctrl`, thread it through
`DialThrough`/`connect`/`pendingSplice`/`accept`.
- **Failing-first:** a relay Server test asserting a `connect{Paid: 0}` still runs the
  free splice unchanged (backward-compat, RED if the new field breaks the free path),
  and a `connect{Paid: h}` with no resolver installed is REFUSED (RED if an unpaid-but-
  marked connect silently downgrades to free).
- No behavior change to free relay; the marker is inert until Step 3 installs a
  resolver. Mergeable alone.

### Step 3 — the Authorizer seam + the settle-driver (§3)

`adapters/relay` (the `PaidResolver`/`PaidSettler` interfaces + the accept-path branch)
and `core/node` (`ResolveRelayAuthorizer`) and `cmd/silt/daemon.go` (the wiring). This
is the rendezvous.
- **Failing-first (integration, `adapters/relay` + `core/node`):** a paid connect
  resolves to a live node session and runs `SplicePaid`; the forward bytes gate on the
  node's authorizer; at close the settler fires once. A paid connect whose handle the
  fetcher does not own is REFUSED (the `ephID` ownership check on the live path — RED if
  the resolver skips it). The concurrency seam (§3, the resolver called off the node's
  loop) gets a `-race` test.
- This is the heaviest step (it crosses the seam and touches the daemon), so it lands on
  top of the proven marker (Step 2) and the hardened reaper (Step 1).

### Step 4 — the e2e proof (§4)

`e2e/relay_paid_test.go`, three real daemons.
- **Failing-first:** the five assertions of §4. It is RED until Steps 1-3 are all in
  (the rendezvous does not exist without them), so it lands last and proves the whole
  binding on the real network path.

### Why this order

- **Reaper first (1):** cheapest, removes a latent leak, no dependency on the seam.
- **Marker before seam (2 before 3):** the seam's resolver is keyed on the marker's
  handle; the marker is testable (backward-compat + refuse-unresolved) without the node.
- **Seam before e2e (3 before 4):** the e2e exercises the seam; splitting them keeps the
  large daemon-integration surface (Step 4) reviewable against a proven seam (Step 3).
- Each step is independently mergeable and failing-first, so a reviewer sees one concern
  per PR: leak-close, then wire-marker, then the rendezvous, then the network proof.

## Summary of the decisions

- **Routing policy (§1): OPEN in the design; recommend Option B** (paid is a parallel
  opt-in path, free relay unchanged and still rate-capped). It matches the certified
  sender-funded/conserved economics, regresses no connectivity, and adds no new economic
  knob. **FLAGGED for the human** as an economic-mechanism policy: route to Researcher
  for a no-regression cert + human ratification before the binding ships. This is the one
  Batch-3 point that may need ratification.
- **Paid marker (§2):** one optional `Paid uint64` field on the existing `ctrl` connect
  frame, carrying the node's session handle. `omitempty` = backward-compatible; zero =
  free (today's path unchanged). One op, one branch, no new dispatch.
- **Authorizer wiring (§3):** a `PaidResolver` callback the daemon installs on the relay
  Server, resolving `(fetcher, handle)` to the node-owned `Authorizer` via
  `Node.ResolveRelayAuthorizer` (reusing the `ephID`-ownership check). The verifier and
  M0 guards stay in `core/node`; the adapter stays a dumb byte pump. Settlement fires
  once at close, driven by the live pump's return (a `PaidSettler` callback →
  `SettleRelaySession`). Flagged concurrency seam: the resolver is called off the node's
  event loop and must be synchronized.
- **Reaper teardown (§3b):** `sweepRelaySeen` calls `sess.closeSession()` on each reaped
