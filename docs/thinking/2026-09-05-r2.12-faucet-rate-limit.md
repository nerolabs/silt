# 2026-09-05 — R2.12: the faucet rate limit (local admission control on the starter grant)

- **Date:** 2026-09-05 · **Seat:** BUILDER · **Branch:** `builder/r2.12-faucet-rate-limit`
- **Base:** `origin/main` = `d99509a` (PR #744 merged)
- **Owner calls already made (2026-09-03, stand):** `grant = 500_000`; rate-limit the faucet;
  `RequireBondedFetchers` default OFF. **Sequencing rule accepted 2026-09-05:** R2.12 lands at or
  before priced delivery goes live (the Economist's condition on the `grant/r` pin; the
  Researcher's G-BB-19 certification made it a named condition).

**Context / trigger.** Every fresh identity that touches a server's ledger is handed a 500,000
starter grant at `Register` (`core/credit/credit.go:394`). Identities are free (a keypair), so
the grant is an unbounded mint of balance: the guard-fill grief costs 6,554 fresh grants and is
"one funded identity away" today (Economist §2.3); the relay lane's shared guard can be filled by
a faucet-funded flood of relay opens (`R-GUARD-SHARED-FILL`, R2.14); and once delivery is priced
(R2.9) `N × grant/r` is the high-side subsidy the pin cannot bound alone. R2.12 bounds `N`.

**Evidence (per build-immutable #7):**
- `core/credit/credit.go:391-400` — `Register` mints the grant on first touch; `acct()` calls
  `Register` on EVERY path (serve, audit, bond challenge, bounty, escrow, relay, and every
  read-side getter), so a limiter AT `Register` would break unrelated accounting, and because
  `Register` is idempotent a denial there is a PERMANENT exclusion of an honest fetcher
  (Economist §2.1). Both are why the grant must be a separate, retryable step.
- `core/credit/invariant_a_test.go:46-135` — every exported `*Ledger` method must be classified
  `mints | reduces | neutral`; the build fails on an unclassified one. The faucet's methods are
  balance-only and classify `neutral`; that classification is what makes the rate Evolving-tier
  (the Economist's three-part test, §2.4: no proof cites it, no consensus rule reads it, its
  failure is spam/inflation not an M0 corner).
- `core/credit/delivery.go:60-105` — the derived guard cap: `W × EpochBlocks ×
  maxServeTrackedPerBlock`, floored at 65,536. The economist's cliff: steady guard occupancy
  from `N` new identities per epoch is `50N`; binds at `N = 1,311`; at `N = 100` it is 0.076×.
- `core/node/tokenrole.go:146` — the precedent for a wall-interval rule in core: token-issue
  dedup keyed on `n.clock.Now()` against a TTL. `ports.MonotonicNanos` (`ports/ports.go:206-217`)
  is the type for a source that cannot be stepped; `cmd/silt` already builds one from
  `time.Since` (tagged, for the census); the faucet needs an UNTAGGED one.
- **No block interval `T_b` is measured anywhere in the tree or the docs** (grep: none). The
  economist's rate is denominated per EPOCH (`DerivedEpochBlocks = 8`,
  `cmd/silt/daemon.go:2062`). The limiter is required to key on a local monotonic clock, NEVER
  the ledger's epoch watermark (poisonable, F8) nor `chainEpoch()` (a chainless node would be
  denied the faucet forever). So the refill interval must be a WALL duration, and its
  epoch-equivalent is unknowable until `T_b` is measured — that is the one number this build
  cannot derive.
- `credit.New` sites: `cmd/silt/daemon.go:657` (grant 500,000), `sim/economy.go:78` (grant = one
  publish), three sims with grant 0. 34 test files call `Register` and 21 assert on the 500,000
  grant; the default-build behaviour of an UNCONFIGURED ledger must therefore stay byte-for-byte.

**Options weighed:**

*Where the grant is applied.*
- **(A) Lazily, at first touch, inside `acct()` — RECOMMENDED.** `Register` creates a
  zero-balance account; `acct()` then calls `Grant(id)`, which applies the starter grant iff the
  identity has not been granted and the bucket admits. A denied identity is `grant pending`: it
  keeps a zero balance and is retried on its next touch, so a denial is never permanent. Every
  path that today receives a grant at first touch still does — the node's own account, a
  repairer paid a bounty, a fetcher — only the RATE is new. Semantics for an UNCONFIGURED
  ledger (no bucket) are unchanged, so sims and the 34 test files stand.
- **(B) Explicitly, at named sites only (the serve path and the node's own start).** Sharper,
  but it changes who gets a grant (a repairer paid a bounty would not), which re-prices
  sims and fixtures the owner has not asked to re-price. Rejected for this build; (A) makes
  it a one-line move later.

*What the rate keys on.*
- **(C) The ledger's epoch watermark or `chainEpoch()`** — refuted by the Economist (§2.2) and
  I concur from the code: the watermark is peer-influenced (F8) and a chainless pony has epoch 0
  forever. Rejected.
- **(D) An injected `ports.MonotonicNanos`, lazily refilled token bucket — RECOMMENDED.**
  Capacity `C` grants, refill `C` per interval `T`, computed on read from `now()`; no
  goroutine, deterministic under a fake source. The daemon injects `time.Since(start)`
  (monotonic); sims inject their sim clock.

*The interval `T`, the number this build cannot derive.*
- **(E) A flag, `-grant-interval`, with a default on the CONSERVATIVE side — RECOMMENDED,
  default put to the Economist for advice.** Denials are retryable and liveness-only, so
  erring toward a LONGER interval (stricter) costs an honest cohort join a wait, while erring
  shorter loosens the grief bound. Epochs are minutes-scale on every tier that has committed
  blocks (the e2e objective-consensus commit lands in ~30 s), so "100 per hour" is stricter
  than "100 per epoch" by at least an order of magnitude and the grief bound tightens
  accordingly (6,554 grants ≥ 66 hours). Proposed default: capacity 100, refill 100 per
  1 hour. Re-derivation trigger: the first measured `T_b` (a standing measurement already
  owed), and the Economist's own: two consecutive intervals with `grantsDenied > 0` against
  honest arrivals. The assumption is named on the flag and in the wire telemetry.

*Telemetry.*
- `grantsIssued`, `grantsDenied` (identities currently grant-pending, not touch events — a
  pending identity retried 1,000 times is one denial), `bucketLevel`, and the configured
  `capacity`/`intervalSec`, on `GET /api/status` under a `faucet` object. **`grantsIssued` is
  the count of fresh identities this ledger has funded — a census-adjacent number, and the
  honest arrival rate the R2.9a run exists to measure.** It goes behind the `-privacy` clause
  (`countersWithheld`) like every other node-wide counter; the operator's tokened read has it.

**Decision + rationale:** (A) + (D) + (E). It is the smallest change that makes the faucet
bounded (the ratified call), keeps every denial retryable (the Economist's hard requirement),
changes nothing for an unconfigured ledger, and keeps the one un-derivable number an explicit,
labelled, Evolving flag rather than a constant that pretends to know `T_b`.

**What would change my mind:** the Economist advising a different default or a burst capacity
larger than one interval's refill (a cohort join at a pony); the PE ruling that lazy granting
inside `acct()` is the wrong seam; a measured `T_b`.

**Gates planned (untagged, `core/credit` + `cmd/silt` + `e2e`):** bucket arithmetic under a
fake source (fill, drain, refill, no over-refill); a denied identity is PENDING and is granted
on a later touch after refill — never permanently excluded; `Register` yields a zero balance
and `Grant` the 500,000, once; an unconfigured ledger grants every first touch exactly as
before (the existing 34 files are that gate); Invariant-A classification of the three new
methods; the daemon refuses a non-positive capacity or interval; `/api/status` `faucet`
telemetry present tokened and withheld untokened under `-privacy`; the grief bound computed
from the constants (`6,554 grants / capacity` intervals ≥ 66); a live-daemon e2e arm where the
101st distinct fetcher in one interval is pending and funded after the interval.

## v2 — the reconciled design (built)

**What the two reviews agreed on, and the code now does:**
- **The seam is the SPEND gates, not `acct()`.** Option (A) was refuted with no attacker: the
  bond-audit sweep calls `RecordBondChallenge` and `Reputation` for every bonded peer every
  60 s (`core/node/bondaudit.go:243,271`, `peerBonds` capped at 4,096), so a 100-token bucket
  emptied on the first sweep of any swarm above 100 peers — and it is the same seam the tree
  rejected for the R2.9a stamp ("first touch is not first fetch", G-BB-24). Now: `Register`
  creates a ZERO-balance account marked `grantPending` ON THE ACCOUNT (no side set — a
  pending-set would be grow-only, the `validatorsSeen` trap); the grant is attempted only at
  `CanPublish`, `ChargePublish` and `FundEscrow`, the three balance-reading gates
  (`credit.go`, `escrow.go`), and nowhere else. A fetcher, a PoR prover, a bounty-paid
  repairer and a bond-challenged peer never needed a balance and take no token.
- **The node's own identity is granted UNMETERED**, once, at daemon start, before the ledger
  is attached (`GrantOwner`; PE S7, Economist §3.3).
- **Continuous accrual**, not a periodic top-up: tokens accrue at `refill/interval` with the
  sub-token remainder carried in integer nanoseconds (`faucet.go`); a cohort of 120 at a
  100-bucket waits minutes, not an hour boundary (Economist §2.1).
- **The constructor never builds a bucket that cannot refill** (PE S5: a zero interval
  drained once and denied for 104 simulated days): non-positive inputs yield nil = UNLIMITED;
  the operator-facing refusal lives in `cmd/silt`.
- **No shipped default.** The limiter is OFF unless BOTH `-grant-capacity` and
  `-grant-per-hour` are set; OFF is byte-for-byte the pre-R2.12 faucet. The one number this
  build could not derive is refused-until-set, not invented (PE S4, build-immutable #7).
- **The start-up assertion** `capacity × (grant/fee) × (W+1) ≤ MaxPaidSerial/4` refuses a
  capacity whose worst-case guard occupancy exceeds a quarter of the paid-serial cap
  (Economist §2.3). It ties four constants in three packages, and it is the only thing that
  catches the `grant/r` composition break automatically: raising `grant` toward the 64 GiB
  pin fails at start-up instead of silently opening the guard.
- **Deny AND degrade are both built**, selected by `-grant-deny-floor` (0 = deny: stay pending;
  N = degrade: receive N once — the PE recommends exactly one fee). **The owner picks.**
- **Telemetry** (`credit.FaucetStats` → `/api/status` `faucet`): configured, capacity,
  perInterval, intervalSec, denyFloor, level, grantsIssued, grantsDegraded, grantsPending
  (DISTINCT pending identities — a retry is not a new denial). It is **not** the R2.9a arrival
  series (it counts spenders at the spend gates; the census counts fetchers at
  `recordFetched`) and is not positioned as one (PE S8a, Economist §4.3). Withheld with
  `stats` under `countersWithheld` for an unauthenticated reader on a `-privacy=on` node; a
  reader that cannot see it can still PROBE the bucket remotely with fresh keys (PE S8c) —
  residual `R-FAUCET-BUCKET-PROBEABLE`, coupled to `R-BB-CENSUS-*`, not closed by withholding.
- **What R2.12 is and is not.** It bounds `dN/dt`, the RATE of fresh grants, never `N`: a
  denial defers a grant and a patient farm recovers every deferred one (PE S2). It is a
  soft, disclosed deterrent standing in for the unbuilt structural cost of identity
  (`docs/network-durability.md` §4/§5, consulted per build-immutable #5), and it is labelled
  so in the code and the flag text. Build-immutable #4 is engaged: an attacker holding the
  bucket at zero with free identities raises the honest onboarding floor to infinity under
  "deny"; that is exactly the trade the deny/degrade call prices.

**Where the two reviews disagreed, and how it is carried:**
- **`T_b`.** The PE: block production is demand-driven (`chainrole.go:676-684`), so `T_b` is
  not a constant and "epochs are minutes-scale" inverts on a quiet swarm. The Economist: three
  cloudtest observations bracket it at 40–170 s/block on publish-driven topologies, and the
  ratified 100/epoch is therefore 265–1,125/hour, with 265/hour the adversarially-conservative
  reading → **256/hour, capacity 256** (13.6× guard margin at the slowest observed cadence).
  Both are right about different things: the bracket is real but load-dependent. The build
  ships NO default (PE) and prints the Economist's derivation and its assumption at start-up
  when the operator sets the flags; **whether 256/256 becomes the shipped default is the
  owner's**, put to them with both positions.
- **Tier.** The PE: the Evolving-tier claim's part 1 failed the day the G-BB-19 certification
  named R2.12 a condition of the `grant/r` pin and read `N × grant/r`. Routed to the
  Researcher with that sentence attached (Q1 of
  `R2.12-faucet-rate-tier-and-grant-ratio-composition-RESEARCH-CERTIFICATION-2026-09-05.md`);
  nothing in the code claims a tier.

**New residuals named by the reviews, NOT closed by this build:**
- `R-FAUCET-ACCOUNT-MAP-UNBOUNDED` — `Register` still appends to `l.order`/`l.accounts`
  without bound, reachable free by any one-byte fetch; R2.12 bounds the mint, not the map.
- `R-FAUCET-RESTART-REGRANT-HERD` — the ledger is ephemeral, so every restart re-grants the
  whole population; the bucket is in-memory and resets FULL so the herd is absorbed at the
  burst; re-examine when FP-2 re-arms.
- `R-FAUCET-BUCKET-PROBEABLE` — above.
- The Economist's §5.3: at `grant/r = 64 GiB` realised by RAISING `g`, one grant is ~73× the
  guard cap and no rate bounds occupancy; the start-up assertion refuses that configuration,
  and WHICH constant moves (G-BB-30) is with the Researcher (Q2).

**The two owner calls this build surfaces (both shapes built; flags carry them):**
1. **Default:** ship OFF (refused-until-set; the PE) or ship 256 grants / 256 per hour ON (the
   Economist's derivation, assumption printed). Recommendation: 256/256 ON — the PE's
   objection was to an INVENTED number, and the Economist's is derived with its margin
   carried in the one weakly-measured variable, which is the honest way to carry it.
2. **Empty bucket:** deny (stay pending) or degrade to one fee (50,000). Recommendation: the
   PE's — degrade to one fee. It keeps the build-immutable #4 floor structurally non-zero
   (the property an attacker cannot take away) and still removes 90 % of a farm's
   per-identity yield; the high side is carried where the certification already puts it, by
   `grant/r` and detection, not by a counter the adversary's own traffic moves.
