# 2026-09-06 — R2.9 build: the delivery lane's anchor quantization at the ratified price, and what to build before it is certified

**Context / trigger:** R2.9 (byte-denominated per-increment delivery settlement, `D-R2.9-DIRECTION`) is the
next build. Its build questions were certified on 2026-09-04 at the provisional price `(U, p) = (4,096, 4,097)`;
that price was REFUTED and re-pinned to `(262,144, 1)` by G-R212-7 on 2026-09-06, which also opened **G-R212-8**:
R2.9 must state the delivery lane's session/anchor quantization and prove one grant composes to `B_floor`
without burning a face remainder. This entry records what the re-price changed in the build shape, the one
question it left open, and the split between what is built now and what waits.

**Evidence (build-immutable #7):**
- The price: `core/credit/numeraire.go:32-35` (`DeliveryIncrementBytes = 262_144`, `DeliveryIncrementCredit = 1`);
  the face `core/relaypay/wire.go:34` (`ShippedAnchorFace = 50_000`). One face funds `⌊f/p⌋·U =
  13,107,200,000 B = 12.21 GiB` at ONE server (G-R212-7 cert §4.1).
- The spend timing R2.9 adopts is the relay lane's: `core/credit/relayanchor.go:89-143` (`SpendRelayAnchors`:
  all-or-nothing into the shared guard, budget = Σ face), `core/node/relayrole.go:440-459` (open admits on a
  non-zero face), `core/node/relaytransport.go:104-124` (settle once, delete the handle, pay `min(count, budget)`,
  burn the remainder).
- Today's witnessed leg is FLAT: `core/credit/delivery.go:501-530` pays `fee − skim` per receipt and the

- The fetch path is multi-provider: a column's shards are pulled from whichever provider answers

- The guard cap is derived from a per-block serve COUNT (`delivery.go:98-108`, `maxServeTrackedPerBlock = 256`).
  The R2.9 cert §4.2 re-derives it from bytes per anchor; at 48 KiB per anchor `T_b` was load-bearing (§4.3).
- Blocks are proposed on demand, not on a clock (`core/node/chainrole.go:719,775,1338,1889` — publishes,
  revocations, rounds, bond drains), so `T_b` is a property of a running topology, not a constant.

## What the re-price changed in the certified build shape

| Certified at `(4,096, 4,097)` | At `(262,144, 1)` | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| one anchor funds 48 KiB; `k_max_delivery = 12` per open; top-up is the main path because one anchor is too SMALL for one object | one anchor funds 12.21 GiB; `k_max_delivery = ⌈S_max·p/f⌉ = 1` (the relay lane's T-RELAY-GRAN shape) | top-up within a session is no longer the affordability path; the hazard inverts to one anchor being too LARGE for one server's share |
| delivery guard population = serves / 48 KiB; the 65,536 floor holds only if `T_b ≲ 6 s` at 100 Mbps | population = serves / 12.21 GiB; at gigabit and `T_b = 3,600 s` the honest live set is `40·3600·125 MiB/s / 12.21 GiB ≈ 1,440` entries | the floor dominates by 45× at an hour-long block interval; `T_b` is no longer load-bearing for the cap (it stays owed for R-REAPER-FORFEIT's reap window) |
| G-R212-8 counts faces per OBJECT: 44.7 GiB needs 3.66 faces of a 10-face grant | a face is spent at ONE server; an object fetched from `n` providers opens `n` sessions | the per-object count is not the binding constraint — the per-SERVER remainder is |

**The open question, with numbers.** Under the certified relay-shaped rule (one session per (fetcher, server);
remainder burned at settle), a `B_floor = 44.7 GiB` object fetched from 20 providers puts ≈ 2.24 GiB
(≈ 8,950 credits) through each session and burns ≈ 41,050 credits of each face: 20 faces = 1,000,000 credits
= two lifetime grants (`R-BB-GRANT-NOT-RENEWABLE`). The grant/r pin (`GrantOverRPinBytes`, 64 GiB) is
computed as if the grant were spendable by the byte; G-R212-8 was opened to check exactly this class of error,
and its stated lift (i) — "an object fetch may span ≥ 4 anchor sessions" — bounds the per-object count, not the
per-server burn. Whether the pin composes therefore depends on the session's quantization rule, which is an
economic mechanism (research gate; `.claude/CLAUDE.md`).

**Options weighed (for the quantization rule — NOT decided here; routed):**
- **(A) One session per (fetcher, server, object); remainder burned at settle (the relay rule verbatim).**
  Simplest; the certified shape. Burns up to one face per provider per object. Fails the pin under any
  multi-provider fetch wider than `g/f = 10` providers per lifetime. Forecloses nothing; costs affordability.
- **(B) One long-lived session per (fetcher, server) spanning objects; settle-once at close or reap.** No new
  burn per object; the face is consumed across every object the fetcher pulls from that server. Costs: the
  server's payment is deferred to session close (its provisional self-mint covers the interval and is reversed
  at settle); the reaper's window (`T_b`-derived, owed) bounds the deferral; per-object accounting needs the
  receipt to carry a running count rather than a per-object one. Privacy: no new linkage beyond today's
  durable-signed receipt. Needs the settle-once and reap semantics re-stated for a session that outlives objects.
- **(C) Sub-face change-making: the server returns an unspent-remainder credential at settle.** Restores byte-
  granular spending. A new blind-signed credential kind (a re-issue under the server's key for the remainder),
  a new token-format constant, and a new spend-once population: the "fifth thing" the R2.9 cert refused for the
  domain. Research-gated in its own right.
- **(D) A smaller face for the delivery lane.** REFUTED by ONE-FACE (the issuer signs opaque blinded bytes and
  cannot see the domain; `relayanchor.go:39-44`). Not an option.

**Decision (this session):** route the quantization question to the Researcher blind (the artifact + the
numbers above + the question; no preferred option), with an Economist advisory in parallel on the grant
budget under (A)/(B). While the verdict is pending, build the half of R2.9 that is INVARIANT to it:

1. **Ledger half (`core/credit`)** — `SpendDeliveryAnchors` (the relay spend with the server recorded on the guard
   entry), a count-denominated settlement paying `min(j·p, budget)` less skim with a PER-INCREMENT reversal of
   the provisional lane (`λ·min(j·U, B)` in accumulator bytes, the un-acknowledged tail kept as the bilateral
   fallback), the remainder burned, the flat `fee − skim` leg retired behind the new entry point. Gates B-1, B-2,
   B-3, B-6, B-14 from the R2.9 cert §5, re-expressed at the ratified price.
2. **The guard cap re-derived from bytes per anchor** (B-4 unit half): the cap is computed in code from
   `bytes_per_anchor = ⌊f/p⌋·U`, `(W+1)·E`, a target serve rate and a `T_b` upper bound, floored at 65,536;
   the gate proves the floor dominates at gigabit and `T_b = 3,600 s`, which is the evidence that the
   measurement is not load-bearing for the cap. `maxServeTrackedPerBlock = 256` is retired as the unit.
3. **The constants table (B-5) and the delivery-side `k_max` derivation** in `cmd/silt` (the one package that
   imports both `core/credit` and `core/relaypay`).

What WAITS for the verdict: the node half (the open message, receipt v3's binding, top-up vs. long-lived
session, settle-once and reap semantics, the fetch-path integration, the swarm driver, the e2e fixture) and
the S5 affordability line (its wording depends on the rule).

**Pressure-test:**
- *Is building the ledger half before the verdict a guess?* No: every option (A)–(C) settles a session by
  `min(j·p, budget)` with a per-increment reversal and a burned remainder (G-1…G-6 are ratified and
  option-independent). What differs is when a session closes and what a receipt binds — node-side.
- *Does retiring `T_b` from the cap contradict the cert?* The cert's rule is "the cap must dominate the honest
  live set" (§4.1); the gate proves domination at a `T_b` bound three orders above any plausible interval. The
  derivation stays in code with `T_b` as a named input, so a future re-price re-derives instead of re-pinning.
- *Why not build (A) now and fix later?* Because the session shape is what the node half, the receipt format,
  and the e2e all encode; building (A) and then moving to (B) rewrites all three. Waiting costs one research
  turn; guessing costs the rewrite.
